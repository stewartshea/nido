## What it is

Someone else's server running the software from this repository, **hosted in
Canada** — your family's records stay on Canadian hardware, under Canadian
privacy law. That is the entire difference, and it is the whole point: it exists
so that using Nido does not require running Docker, patching a host, or
remembering to take a backup.

Sign up at [my.nido-app.ca](https://my.nido-app.ca) with an email and a
password. The only emails you will ever get are about your account — no
marketing, ever. The details are in the [privacy policy](./privacy/).

## What you give up

Running it yourself is the better option when you can, and this page will not
pretend otherwise:

- **The data is on someone else's disk.** The operator holds it, and
  backs it up on their schedule rather than yours.
- **A family database is keyed by the host's master key.** You never see it, so
  you cannot take an independent copy of the key — which is the one thing the
  [self-hosting guide](./self-host/) insists you do. Backups and restores are
  the operator's responsibility, not yours.
- **It is a service, so it can go away.** A self-hosted install is on hardware
  you control and has no expiry. My Nido is a favour, not a guarantee, and
  it is run by one person in their evenings.

:::caution[Do not treat My Nido as your only copy]
My Nido is a convenience. If your family's records matter to you, a
self-hosted instance you control is the durable answer — see
[Self-hosting →](./self-host/). The same export and restore endpoints exist in
both, so you can move between them.
:::

## Is it really the same software?

Yes. My Nido runs builds of
[stewartshea/nido](https://github.com/stewartshea/nido), and every feature
listed in the [documentation index](./index/#what-nido-does) is present in
both. There is no hosted build of Nido, no feature flag that only one side can
see, and no separate codebase.

If My Nido is ever ahead of or behind a release, that is a deployment lag,
not a product difference.

## Supporting the project

Using My Nido is free and always will be. If it is useful to you and you
want to give something back, there is an optional way to do that alongside
your account.

It is not a tier. It does not change what you can do, and nothing about your
account changes if you never use it. The same options are open whether you
self-host or not — see [Support Nido →](./support/) for where the money goes and
what it buys (maintainer time, and nothing else).
